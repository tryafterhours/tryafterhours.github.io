Is office dating allowed in India?

Short answer: yes, in general. No Indian law stops two consenting adults who work together from dating. Office dating, also called workplace dating or corporate dating, is common, and plenty of couples met at work.

It's also more common in India than almost anywhere else. In a 2025 YouGov survey of 13,581 adults across 11 countries, 4 in 10 Indians said they had dated a colleague or were dating one. That was the second-highest of all the countries surveyed, just behind Mexico. In the US, UK and Canada, it was around 3 in 10. (source)

But "legal" and "fine at your company" aren't always the same thing. Here's what to know.

This is general information, not legal advice. Your company's own policy is what applies to you.

What the law says

There's no law in India that bans consensual relationships between coworkers. What the law cares about is consent and conduct.

The main law to know is the POSH Act (the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013). It's about unwelcome behaviour of a sexual nature, not about relationships both people want. Organisations with 10 or more employees must have an Internal Committee to handle complaints.

What this means in practice:

  • A relationship both people want is not harassment.
  • Pursuing someone who has said no, or who clearly isn't interested, can be.
  • If a relationship ends badly, behaviour after the break-up still has to be respectful.

What company HR policies usually say

Many Indian companies, especially larger corporate employers and IT services firms, have a policy on workplace relationships. They commonly say:

  • Disclose it if there's a reporting line. Dating your manager, or someone who reports to you, creates a conflict of interest. Many companies ask you to tell HR so one of you can be moved.
  • Keep it out of work. No favouritism, no public displays, and no letting it affect decisions.
  • Some roles have stricter rules. For example, people who handle appraisals, hiring or sensitive data.

A few companies discourage office relationships altogether, so check your employee handbook or HR portal.

How to keep workplace dating respectful

  • Never date in your reporting line. It's the clearest line almost every policy draws, and it protects both of you.
  • Take no for an answer, the first time. At work you'll see each other tomorrow, so pressure is never okay.
  • Keep it private until you're both ready. Colleagues don't need to know early on.
  • Plan for the break-up while things are good. Agree that whatever happens, you'll stay professional.
  • Don't use work tools to flirt. Company chat and email belong to your employer.
